Work

Bizotico

A legacy watch distributor's first storefront, 25 international brands, engineered to sell online.

Lead Product Designer · Shopify, mobile-first · 2024

Overview

The first online store in the distributor's 25-year history.

Role. Lead Product Designer: information architecture, product and collection UX, mobile-first UI, the Shopify build, and the design system.

Client. Bizotico, an importer and distributor carrying more than 25 international watch brands.

Platform. Shopify, mobile-first, with roughly 70 percent of buyers on a phone. Live since 2024.

[ IMAGE: Bizotico storefront, product page and mobile home ]

The challenge

Sell a 20 to 40 thousand rupee watch to a first-time online buyer.

A 25-year-old distributor with more than 25 international brands and over 600 retail partner stores, and no online store, while 70 percent of Indian watch buyers had already moved online.

01. Multi-brand, one storefront. More than 25 brands across very different price tiers had to share a store, without the premium lines feeling cheapened or the fashion lines feeling niche.

02. A confidence problem, not a price problem. About one in five Indian buyers treats a watch as an investment. What blocks the purchase online is trust, not the number.

03. Mobile is the store. About 70 percent of traffic is on a phone. Designing for the desktop-boutique feel would have served the wrong 30 percent.

Approach

Not "build a store." Design for purchase confidence.

Category research. India's watch market was 3.2 billion dollars in 2024, heading to 5.1 billion by 2033. Luxury-watch e-commerce is globally the least-developed channel. Buyers were ready. Distribution had not followed.

Portfolio audit. Mapped all 25 brands by price band and positioning. That map became the input for the navigation model.

Competitive teardown. Marketplace listings against brand-owned stores, cataloguing exactly where trust breaks on a high-value product page.

Journey mapping. Located the moments of doubt on the path to checkout, and designed a countermeasure for each one.

[ IMAGE: brand portfolio mapped by price band, feeding the navigation model ]

Key decisions

Three decisions that shaped the store.

01

Brand-led information architecture.

Information architecture

Navigable by brand and by watch type, Swiss Made, Automatic, and so on, not one flat grid.

Why

The distributor's value is curation across brands. The IA had to make that visible.

02

The product page as an anxiety-reducer.

Product page

Warranty, manufacturer details, authenticity, EMI, care, and returns, surfaced through progressive disclosure.

Why

The page answers "can I trust this?" before it asks for the sale.

03

A mobile-first system.

Mobile

Condensed navigation, filter and sort, WhatsApp support, a sticky offer bar, and a thumb-reachable add-to-cart.

Why

Built for the 70 percent, not the 30 percent.

Curation is the product. The interface had to make 25 years of it visible.

Design system and build

A system the client can run without a developer.

Component library. Product page, collection, and brand-page templates in one themeable system.

Palette. Deep maroon, black, white. One accent, used on actions and trust badges only.

Built in Shopify. Components map one to one to theme sections. A new brand is a configuration, not a rebuild.

Handoff. Design through to the Shopify build, owned end to end, not thrown over a wall.

Outcomes

The first online revenue in the company's 25 years.

Early-stage store, roughly 30 orders a month. Read these as a first signal, not a verdict.

7.85x return on ad spend on the launch campaign. Roughly 53,000 rupees of spend against 4,16,131 rupees in net sales in May 2024.

First digital revenue channel. Zero to one, built, live, and taking orders.

25 brands online, each with its own presence under one system.

Self-serve catalog and campaigns. The client ships changes without developer support.

Team split. I owned UX, UI, and the Shopify build. Performance marketing and content production were run by teammates, so the store result is shared.

[ IMAGE: Shopify analytics, May 2024, orders, net sales, ROAS ]

Reflection

What I would do differently.

01. Tighter template tiers from day one. Brand-led IA was the right call but maintenance-heavy. A stricter tier system from the start would have cut per-brand setup time significantly.

02. Instrument the trust layer. The product-page reassurance model is the strongest part of the work. Next time I would measure it, checkout drop-off, return rate, instead of asserting it.

03. Use the 600 stores harder. The offline footprint is a bigger trust asset than the first release treated it as. The store locator and the offline-to-online bridge were under-built.
