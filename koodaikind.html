Koodai Kind

A mobile-first Shopify storefront for the South Indian koodai, the product leading every page.

Product Designer and Design Lead · Bleed and Margins · Mobile-first Shopify

Overview

Make the koodai as shoppable and desirable as any modern bag.

Role. Product designer and design lead: the Shopify storefront, mobile-first UI and UX, the colour system, and the bindu page-loader motion.

Client. Koodai Kind, a contemporary brand for the wire-woven koodai bag, made in Coimbatore. Founder-led.

Platform. Shopify, mobile-first, with a pre-order flow. Live at koodaikind.com, tote at ₹4,750.

Campaign photography and art direction were also delivered by the studio.

[ IMAGE: Koodai Kind homepage on laptop and phone ]

The challenge

Sell it as a product, not as rescue-the-artisan.

The koodai is a solved object, durable, light, packable, that fell out of cultural view, not out of usefulness. A new generation is picking it up by choice, and the buyer discovers it on a phone.

01. Reframe, do not reinvent. The koodai does not need redesigning. It needs to be presented as a contemporary choice.

02. Guilt and pride is patronising. The default narrative of heritage-craft branding talks down to the object and to the buyer.

03. Indian objects enter global style. Prada's 2024 market bags were compared to koodais, not the other way round.

Most heritage-craft brands sell the koodai as nostalgia. Koodai Kind wanted to sell it as a bag.

Approach

Treat it as a contemporary commerce problem.

Research base. A generational shift toward buying the koodai as preference, not nostalgia (Elle India, 2025, on the koodai trend). The guilt-and-pride default of heritage-craft branding. Indian objects arriving in global style as originals.

Brief as received. A storefront and campaign that sell the koodai on its design credentials, wire-woven, spill-resistant, light, without using heritage as the reason to buy.

The reframe. Mobile-first product page, a colour system taken from the weaves, photography in real settings, and let the object lead every page.

What shipped kept more heritage voice than the brief asked for. See Outcomes.

Key decisions

Three decisions that let the product lead.

01

Mobile-first product page where the image leads.

Product page

Product pages open on the bag in use. Weave, lining, and structure notes sit below. Built for someone finding the brand on a phone.

Trade-off

A buyer who wants full material detail up front has to scroll for it.

02

A colour system built from the weaves.

Colour system

The colour selector and site accents are drawn from the actual bag stripe colours. Photography is shot in context, not on white.

Trade-off

Less neutral than a clean catalogue, and it ties the UI palette to the current range, so new colourways need palette work.

03

The bindu page-loader.

Motion

A dot expanding into concentric rings before the storefront loads, from the founder's S.H. Raza reference. Presence before commerce.

Trade-off

A load-time gate on a site where speed converts. The bet is that the register it sets is worth the friction. Confirm it shipped.

Let the object carry desire. The interface gets out of the way.

Design system and build

A mobile-first kit, colour from the weave.

System. Colour tokens taken from the weave palette, editorial type, a mobile-first component set for the product page, colour selector, and collection grid, and the bindu loader as a brand motion primitive.

Platform. Shopify, with a pre-order flow and mobile commerce as the priority.

Maintainable. The colour-selector and product-page components are reusable per SKU. Pre-order settings are client-managed.

Outcomes

What shipped, and the gap to be honest about.

Shipped. A live Shopify storefront with pre-order, mobile-first UI, a colour system, and campaign photography. Live at koodaikind.com, tote at ₹4,750.

The gap. The brief was no heritage as pitch, but the site that shipped leads with heritage: "Carried by generations," "Made in Coimbatore," Tamil naming. The anti-nostalgia positioning is not what the live brand does. It looks like a founder call.

This case study claims. The storefront, UI, colour, and motion work, not the zero-nostalgia framing from the source brief.

Not yet known. No sales, pre-order, or traffic numbers. Whether the bindu loader shipped as designed. Any press pickup for the brand specifically.

Reflection

What I would do differently.

01. Reconcile the brief with the founder earlier. "No heritage as pitch" and the heritage-forward voice that shipped should have been one agreed direction, not two.

02. Treat the bindu loader as a performance trade-off. Test it against load time and bounce, not carry it purely as a brand idea.

03. Set success metrics with the founder at kickoff. Pre-orders, add-to-cart, mobile conversion, so the launch has a scoreboard.

One agreed direction beats a clever brief the site does not follow.
