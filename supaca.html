Supaca

Supaca: a private network for India's supercar owners

Supaca is an app for verified supercar owners in India. Clubs post their drives and events there, and members sign up for them with the car they're bringing.

My role
The whole UI/UX and site architecture: home, events, how admins and members interact, and the layers of club features.
Team
Four to five people. One on outreach and onboarding, two designers including me, and two developers.
Timeline
Started in 2023 to 2024 at Boit Club. Designed up to UI in 2024, only partly finished. Picked back up and launched in 2025.

The problem

Supercar owners in India already had clubs, but no product made for them. Clubs ran on scattered WhatsApp groups. There was no way to check who owned a car. Drives were organised through forwarded messages, expensive cars were sold on trust, and many sat parked for weeks with nowhere to go.

How we found out

I was creative director and visual lead at Boit Club, a supercar club. I spent a lot of time with owners and organisers, and our own members were close to us and very open. I also mapped how real clubs control who gets in: open, private, referral-only and women-only.

From this we wrote five archetypes, each with their frustrations, goals and the jobs they need done:

  • Xavier, the Collector. Wants the most unique cars, with no hassle.
  • Bharath, the Collection Flipper. Goes to events to network, and wants to sell quietly because everyone knows his cars.
  • Sameer, the Professional. A CEO who can finally afford a supercar but is new to the lifestyle.
  • Vivaan, the Young Gun. 21, wants to stand out, and hates hassle.
  • Nico, the Value Seeker. Wants a strong badge at a fair price, and worries about buying a damaged car.

I fixed the structure of the app first, sketched the main flows on paper, and tested the wireframes with owners before any visual design.

What we found

It's about status and peers. All five archetypes wanted to stand out among other owners. Four said other people's opinions shape what they do.

Trust was the gap. Bharath didn't want to deal with dealers or agents. Nico was scared of buying a damaged car. Bharath and Sameer both named trust as a deciding factor.

Privacy matters. Bharath wanted to sell without everyone finding out. Owners want to meet other owners, not an audience.

Drives bring people back. Bharath goes to events to network. Sameer wants to experience the lifestyle but doesn't know much about it yet. Members also asked for a way to keep the group together on the road.

Clubs already exist. The product had to bring them in, not replace them.

What we built

Fewer ways in. We started with five: explorer, enthusiast, owner, club owner and partner. A social wall would have filled fast with spotters and fans, which owners didn't want. We cut it to three: owner, club owner and vendor. Today only owners and club owners can join; vendors come later. The rule is simple: you're verified, or you're waiting.

Verifying the car, not just the person. This is for the trust gap Nico and Bharath described. An owner adds their car and uploads its registration (RC) and insurance. A person reviews every application, and the app stays locked until then. Verified owners get a badge. It's slower to join, on purpose.

Nobody gets put in a club they didn't pick. We could have filled the app on day one by putting every Porsche owner in a city into one Porsche club. We didn't. Real clubs form around an area and who knows whom, so members join by choice or with the club owner's approval. For people like Bharath, privacy comes first: where verified and unverified people meet, nobody's name shows until they choose, and avatars are just initials.

Clubs run themselves. Club owners can start or claim a club and decide who gets in. Different clubs get different feature options. Admins approve members and claims.

Sign up with your car. Much of the app leads to the event page. Members pick which car they're bringing, and "Who's coming" shows each person with a verified badge and a colour tag for their car's brand. Every archetype cares about standing out among peers, and the lineup is where that happens. For someone new like Sameer, the event page is an easy way in.

A live convoy map. Members asked for this during the research. During a drive, everyone shows on a live map with their position and speed, so nobody gets left behind.

A rule against faking it. Early on I flagged the risk of an empty app, and we agreed never to fake a member, an attendee or a listing.

What happened

We've invited more than 80 owners and more than 10 clubs. Right now about 25 owners are active, and more than 10 clubs have registered themselves. Three to four real events were listed in the app, and members signed up for them through it. The first 100 verified owners get their first year free.
