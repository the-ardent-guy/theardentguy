What this is

Five case studies, 1,500 to 2,500 words each

Five finished case studies. Each one follows a project from the first question to the thing that shipped, and each decision in them ends where decisions actually end, which is in a trade-off rather than a win.

Most of them carry a table of what was refused and why. Those tables are usually the most useful page in a write-up and the least read, so a summary of all of them is on this page, below the work.

Where a project has no numbers, it says so. Where a product is no longer online, it says that too. Nothing on this page is estimated, inferred from something adjacent, or borrowed from a neighbouring metric.

What we refused

Short version. Full reasoning sits in each case study.
ProjectWhat was refusedWhat that meant
Produsa Letting the system confirm anything it had not actually done. The alpha with four real owners named the failure: false confirmation. Nearly every breakage was the system claiming something was true when it was not.
Supaca Open sign-up. 340 verified owners and zero fake members, in a country with roughly 3,000 to 6,000 owners of this kind in total. A smaller network, on purpose.
Hasta Subscriptions, and selling through fear. 4.7% free to paid and 850 readings in two months without either one. The two easiest revenue mechanics in this category were the two we did not use.
Hasta Hiding the places where the three traditions disagree. Nine conflicts logged inside the product, one still open. A reader that cites its sources has to show you when its sources disagree.
VIST Labs One voice for two audiences. Banks got a technology company. Growers got their own vocabulary, one level further down the same page. With 89% of US community banks having no plans to serve the industry (RMA, 2024), the first read had to be the sober one.
VIST Labs Reporting numbers that were never measured. No analytics were installed on that build, so there is no result to report, and there is no version of this page where one appears.
Bizotico No refusal table on this one. Not every project produces one. This was six months of commercial work that performed, and it is presented that way.
This site Printing praise nobody has agreed to have printed. Three people have said generous things about this work. Permission to quote them was never confirmed, so three boxes stay empty.

How the work gets made

Standing rules, applied across projects
  1. 01Invent the story, never invent the state.
  2. 02Say what a static site cannot do.
  3. 03Trust is built in the edge cases.
  4. 04Privacy fails closed.
  5. 05A dead button costs more later.
  6. 06Done only when it is done.
