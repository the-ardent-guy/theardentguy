Product Designer and Design Lead · Bleed and Margins · Shopify B2B

Overview

Make a compliance-driven buyer trust the company on sight, before they read a spec.

Role. Product designer and design lead: the UI and UX design system, the B2B storefront UX, and Shopify build direction.

Client. VIST Labs, preservation, decontamination, and packaging technology for plant-based and cannabis operators. Illinois, USA.

Platform. Shopify B2B. On a development URL at the point of capture, with the custom domain pending.

The challenge

Make a compliance buyer trust you on sight.

Illinois cannabis is a multi-billion-dollar regulated market, but its supply chain still looks improvised. VIST sells preservation technology to operators on thin margins, where product quality is the whole business.

01. Packaging losses are real. Poor packaging costs growers roughly six grams of sellable weight per pound to terpene and moisture loss. This is an agency figure and still needs sourcing.

02. The supply chain is not the shelf. The B2B supply chain looks like a 2012 startup next to the polished consumer products it supplies.

03. Looking like pharma is the signal. Modified-atmosphere packaging comes from food and pharma. In preservation, that visual language is a trust cue, not a style choice.

The dominant cannabis look, neon, leaf, streetwear, would actively undercut a science-led product.

Approach

Design for the compliance officer, not the vibe.

Research base. The gap between polished cannabis consumer brands and their improvised supply chain. Preservation science's roots in food and pharma. How a compliance-driven B2B buyer reads visual language.

Brief as received. A science-led preservation and packaging brand for growers and operators, with a Shopify storefront to match.

The reframe. The buyer is protecting a crop, not buying a vibe. Lead with data, use lab and pharma UI conventions as trust shorthand, and keep the language category-neutral so the brand is not boxed into cannabis.

Key decisions

Three decisions that built trust.

01

The product page as a data sheet.

Product page

Product pages lead with specifications, compliance certifications, CE, HACCP and GMP, third-party validation, and volume pricing. Lifestyle imagery is removed.

Trade-off

Cold for a casual visitor. Correct for a buyer who already knows what they need.

02

Pharma and lab UI conventions.

UI system

Monospaced type for data, near-clinical neutrals, and an accent reserved for actions and compliance indicators.

Trade-off

It risks reading as generic B2B. The bet is that restraint reads as competence in a regulated market.

03

Category-coy language.

Positioning

The public site says "plant-based industries" and foregrounds EU and CE compliance over cannabis culture.

Trade-off

It gives up cannabis-native identity and search. It gains credibility with compliance-driven buyers.

In a regulated market, the restrained option is the credible one.

Design system and build

A system that reads like a lab.

System. A neutral palette, monospaced data type paired with a humanist sans for prose, a whitespace scale, and components for spec tables, certification badges, compliance callouts, and action states.

Platform. Shopify, built as a B2B storefront.

Maintainable. Product data drives the spec tables. Certification blocks are reusable. Non-designers can add SKUs without breaking the data-sheet layout.

Reflection

What I would do differently.

01. Settle the positioning before UI. Cannabis versus plant-based. The hedged language changed how much category context every page needed. One decision, up front.

02. Get the domain and analytics in at launch. So there is a baseline to measure the trust-and-conversion bet against.

03. Prototype the data-sheet product page with real growers. "Reads like a data sheet" is still an untested assumption about what that buyer actually wants.
