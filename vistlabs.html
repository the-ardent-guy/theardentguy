Work

VIST Labs, Illinois

One website, two audiences: repositioning VIST Labs for banks and growers.

Product Designer, solo · Bleed and Margins · 3 months

Overview

The brief was one line: look more pharmaceutical.

VIST Labs makes patented post-harvest technology for cannabis growers. CryoPasteurization decontaminates flower without chemicals or radiation. AMAPS packaging keeps it fresh for up to a year. A mobile unit brings both services into the grower's facility.

They came to me for a website redesign. What they actually needed was a repositioning.

[ IMAGE: the old vistlabs.com homepage, fear imagery and a long undifferentiated scroll ]

The problem

Their banks needed to see a technology company. Their customers needed to see a fix for failed microbial tests.

Cannabis is legal in Illinois but still a Schedule I substance federally. Banks that serve cannabis businesses take on heavy compliance work, and 89% of community banks have no plans to serve the industry (RMA, 2024). That single number is the whole design constraint.

The old site served neither audience. It opened with "Cannabis Remediation", named cannabis in every section, and sold through fear: mould close-ups, a lungs image, "Would you inhale that?". Five patented processes sat in one long scroll with no hierarchy.

Problem statement How might we present VIST as a regulated technology company to banks, while keeping it the obvious choice for the growers who buy from it?

Research

Three audiences, each with a different entry point.

Stakeholder sessions. I sat with the founders to understand their apprehension. Their banks needed to see the technology, and the old site read like a cannabis brand. With their help I collected the technical detail on each process and machine, so the site could explain the tech at the level a grower understands.

Desk research. Federal status, FinCEN reporting requirements for banks, and Illinois cannabis banking programmes, to understand how a conservative lender reads a cannabis-adjacent business.

Competitive audit. Rad Source, Willow Industries and Ziel all market openly to cannabis and lean on compliance claims such as 99.9% test pass rates and EU GMP. VIST could not take that route, so compliance and process had to carry the brand.

Content audit. I inventoried the old site's services and claims, and flagged what was missing: certifications, team, commercial terms and technical documents.

Key decisions

Three decisions that let one site speak to a lender and a grower at once.

01

Two-tier messaging.

What happened

The word "cannabis" was doing the opposite job for each audience. It reassured growers and alarmed lenders.

The decision

The homepage speaks to the most conservative reader, using "plant-based industries" and "pharmaceutical-grade standards". Cannabis appears one level down, on a Markets Served page, in terms a grower recognises immediately. I built a vocabulary map so every service had a bank-safe name and a grower-facing meaning.

ServiceHomepage languageWhat a grower reads
CryoPasteurization Natural microbial load reduction Decontamination for cannabis
AMAPS Aseptic modified atmosphere packaging Long-term post-harvest protection
EU GMP alignment Built to international pharma-grade standards Ready for EU medical export
The trade-off The homepage gives up search traffic for "cannabis decontamination". The blog and solution pages pick that up instead.

02

An IA built on problem, solution, commercial model.

What happened

Five patented processes sat in one scroll, and the commercial terms were mixed into the service descriptions.

The decision

The homepage states the problem first, then what VIST does, then how to buy: hero, who we are with three USPs, five solutions, a three-step solution showing how the services work as one system, four buying options, social proof, blog.

Each of the five services got its own product detail page, broken into its USPs, its process, and what the customer gets. At launch every page ended in a consultation request. Microbial testing kits were the first product VIST planned to sell directly, so that page was structured to switch from enquiry to add-to-cart without a redesign. Opportunities covers sales, leases, rentals and crop-year protection; Resources holds the blog, spec sheets and events.

The trade-off One fixed template gives less room to tailor each page to its technology.
[ IMAGE: the CryoPasteurization product page, and the early sketches beside it ]
03

Evidence in place of fear.

What happened

The old site sold through mould close-ups and a lungs image. It worked on growers and disqualified the company with everyone else.

The decision

I replaced the fear imagery with claims a lender or a QA manager can check: 99.9% microbial reduction, no chemicals or radiation, terpene preservation. The homepage gained a social proof block with certifications, awards, client logos, press mentions and team credentials. Photography focuses on the equipment, the mobile unit and the process.

The trade-off The old site's emotional pull on growers is gone. The before and after section on each product page keeps a controlled version of it.

Design language

A deep medical blue, because that is where VIST wanted lenders to place it.

Plain sans-serifs and a deep medical blue match the visual language of pharma and diagnostics sites. The restraint is the trust signal.

Platform and delivery

Shopify over WordPress, for three reasons.

Its native checkout was ready for the microbial kits VIST planned to sell next. Its SEO defaults were solid. And the client could edit content without developer support. I built the product page template as reusable Shopify sections.

The stakeholders were in Illinois, 10 to 11 hours behind me. I ran the project through a Notion client portal with milestones for research, UX, UI, build, domain migration and training, so the founders could review asynchronously.

Outcome

It went live on vistlabs.com and became the client's primary site.

The founders were happy with it and used it with their customers. It was later taken offline because of changes on the business side, unrelated to the site.

Shipped: a positioning and messaging framework, a six-section sitemap with 12 sub-pages, wireframes, a design language, a live Shopify build and client handover.

On measurement

No metrics. I never installed analytics, so I cannot tell you whether the repositioning worked.

What I would have measured: lead quality before and after, the split between bank enquiries and grower enquiries, and whether the Markets Served page was reached by the people it was written for. Without a baseline none of that is recoverable, and the site is down now. This is the clearest lesson I took off this project.

Reflection

What I would do differently.

Install analytics before launch. Without a baseline I cannot show whether the repositioning changed lead quality or volume. See above, and it still bothers me.

Test the language with real readers. The two-tier messaging was validated through the founders only. Ten minutes each with a banker and a grower would have tested it directly, and I did not do it.
