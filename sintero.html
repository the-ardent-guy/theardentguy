Sintero

A specification-grade design system for luxury stone surfaces, one visual language across web, store, and print.

Digital Design Lead · Bleed and Margins · 2024

Overview

One system, from a mobile product page to a printed technical manual.

Role. Digital design lead: information architecture, UI, the shared web and print system, and build direction.

Client. Sintero, luxury sintered stone and large-format porcelain surfaces, Bengaluru.

Platform. Shopify, with CMS-managed collections. Live at sintero.in.

The studio also delivered the brand identity and the print catalogues. My focus was the digital product and the system that tied every touchpoint together.

[ IMAGE: Sintero responsive site on a dark charcoal ground ]

The challenge

Make one system carry technical credibility and luxury.

Sintero has real manufacturing depth and a growing slab collection, but a brand presence that did not match it, selling to specifiers who commit large surface volumes on trust alone.

01. Specified, not bought. In luxury surfaces the product is specified by architects and designers, so brand trust is a technical concern, not just a marketing one.

02. The competitor is unfamiliarity. Sintered stone's advantages are barely known outside the design community. The real rival is not another brand. It is not knowing the material.

03. The catalogue is the proof. A printed catalogue is often the first physical evidence of quality a specifier handles. If it feels cheap, the surface does too.

The brand had to teach and reassure at the same time.

Approach

Not "make the slab look luxurious." Make the system consistent.

Research base. The specification path in luxury interiors, from awareness to catalogue to spec meeting to project order, and how specifiers judge a brand before they ever touch a slab.

Brief as received. Give the brand the presence to sit in rooms alongside large hospitality and residential developers: identity, website, store, catalogues.

The reframe. Not the beauty shot. One grid, one type scale, one level of restraint at every touchpoint, credible on a phone or on an architect's desk.

Key decisions

Three decisions that shaped the system.

01

Built around specification, not the showroom.

Information architecture

The IA leads with collections, project references, and technical resources, routing to a spec-enquiry contact flow rather than a buy-now path.

Trade-off

A homeowner browsing gets a more technical, less lifestyle-led journey. I chose the specifier as the primary user.

02

One modular grid across screen and print.

Design system

Catalogue templates and web product pages share a grid and type scale, so a spec sheet and a product page feel like one brand.

Trade-off

Less editorial freedom in print. In return, a small team maintains both without them drifting apart.

03

Lead with the process, not the finished slab.

Narrative

The homepage and collection intros open on sintering, the making, before the beauty shots.

Trade-off

It delays the room-setting imagery buyers expect. It also separates Sintero from every competitor that opens on a styled interior.

Restraint, held at every touchpoint, is the luxury signal.

Design system and build

One grid, screen to page.

System. Near-black and charcoal with a single warm-gold accent. Serif display, clean body. A shared grid and type scale, and component sets for collections, project references, and resources.

Platform. Shopify, with collections managed in the CMS so the client adds finishes without development.

Maintainable. The same grid and tokens drive the catalogue templates. The client extends collections and swaps project photography themselves.

Outcomes

What shipped, and what we do not know yet.

Shipped. Brand identity, Shopify site and digital store, print catalogues and technical manuals, one system across screen and page. Live at sintero.in.

Client-reported. The client reported that external perception shifted toward subtle, premium, and design-first, and that the change was noticed from clients through to dealers. Quote pending permission.

Unverified. The agency page claims Sintero is now specified into named hotel groups and large developers. This is client-supplied with no sign-off, so no client names or logos appear here.

Not yet known. No traffic, enquiry-volume, or specification-win numbers. No before-and-after baseline. Launch date unconfirmed.

Reflection

What I would do differently.

01. Define the shared grid as one spec, up front. Web and print never need reconciling later if the grid and type tokens are a single source from day one.

02. Set one measurable goal at kickoff. Enquiry rate, catalogue downloads, so the outcome is not only qualitative.

03. Make the spec-request flow the primary conversion. For a specification product the store is an enquiry funnel. Treat it as the main path from day one, not a contact page.
