Produsa

What does it look like when an agentic organisation builds your product for you?

Product Designer · AI Product, UX, Agentic Architecture · 2026

Overview

Type an idea in plain language, watch a live prototype appear, refine it by chatting, ship it to a real URL.

Produsa is an AI app-builder, built in-house at Boit Technologies. I designed the build experience and the interaction patterns that govern how the product behaves as an AI system.

The engine, agent architecture, infrastructure, and deployment, was built by the engineering team. The design decisions below are mine.

[ IMAGE: Produsa build screen, chat on the left, live prototype in the centre ]

The problem

Most AI builders make you watch agents work before you see anything real.

Logs, spinners, research reports. For a first-time or non-technical builder, that dead time kills momentum before the product ever proves itself.

The bet: show the thing immediately, and never show a blank panel.

Key decisions

Three calls that define how the product feels.

01

The panel is always a live prototype, never a log.

What happened

Early versions showed a sequential list of agents working. We cut it.

The decision

The centre panel is always a rendered prototype. Even mid-build, a fading placeholder stands in rather than a progress log. This pushed real engineering complexity downstream, because the render pipeline has to be fast and never break, but time to first impression is the whole thesis.

Show the thing. The wait is not the product.

02

Honesty over confidence in every AI reply.

What happened

The default instinct for an AI assistant is to sound helpful, to say "done" and echo the request back as confirmation.

The decision

The assistant only ever describes edits that actually applied. When part of an edit lands and part does not, it says exactly what happened and what did not. One false "ready" breaks trust permanently in a tool people use to ship real work.

A tool that ships real work cannot afford a confident lie.

03

Design psychology as a gate, not a guideline.

What happened

Generated screens can be attractive and still quietly work against the person using them.

The decision

Every screen the product generates is checked against six behavioural laws: smart defaults, goal-gradient progress, reciprocity before any signup wall, earned investment before registration, real urgency, and contrast-anchored pricing. A screen that fails gets one mandatory revision pass before it ships, the same as a build error.

When a beautiful screen and a psychologically correct screen conflict, the psychology wins.

Outcome

169 registered users, 315 projects created, 122 shipped to a live URL.

Figures are from the most recent audit, at a pre-revenue stage. Monetisation is wired end to end. The current focus is converting shipped projects into paying accounts.

[ IMAGE: usage trajectory, users, projects created, projects shipped ]

What I would flag honestly

The product is ahead of its go-to-market.

It reliably builds and ships things people want, but it has not yet proven that people will pay for it. That gap, not another design pattern, is the next real problem to solve.
