Produsa

Turning a website build into a conversation

Produsa is a product from Produsa Labs. It researches, designs and builds websites for small businesses through a chat, on the web or on WhatsApp.

Context

At Produsa Labs, we set out to run an agency's whole process with AI agents. A website project at an agency follows the same steps every time: research, niche, brand, prototype, build. We turned each step into an agent. Websites came first because that was the process I knew best.

The problem

A good website still costs too much for many small businesses in India, especially outside the big cities. Owners have two options, and neither works for them.

AgenciesWebsite builders
Expensive, with weeks of back-and-forth Expect the owner to make every design decision
Use terms the owner doesn't know English-first and built around templates
The owner can't edit the site afterwards Fixed monthly plans, whether the owner edits or not

How might we give a small business owner a good website they can afford, without asking them to know anything about design?

Users

Owners of small and mid-sized businesses: a bakery, a bearings distributor, a cafe, a steel trader. They run their business on WhatsApp, and many don't work in English. They can describe their business, but they can't describe a website.

  • What they can answer: who their customers are, what they sell, and sites they like.
  • What they can't: layouts, typography, colour systems and information architecture.

So the product asks the first kind of question and makes the second kind of decision for them.

Research: the alpha test

Before launch, we ran a moderated usability test with four owners: a steel trader, a bearings distributor, a cat cafe and a bakery. The discovery questions worked. Three problems kept coming up:

  1. The chat said "done" when nothing had changed. One bakery owner was told three times that her photos were up before they appeared.
  2. The agent misread requests, especially when owners wrote in their own language.
  3. Owners couldn't tell whether a slow build was still running or had stopped.

In all three, the system either gave the owner false feedback or no feedback at all. That shaped most of what I designed after the test.

Design principles

  • Truthful feedback. The chat only confirms what's actually on the page.
  • Visible system status. The owner always knows what's happening and how far along the build is.
  • Zero design questions. The owner shares their business, and the agents make the design decisions.
  • Transparent cost. The cost of every action is visible when the owner takes it.

How it works

The build follows the order an agency works in:

Researchthen Nichethen Brand designthen Prototypethen Website

An art director agent, a content agent and an SEO agent work on each build. Each agent checks its output against written standards and fixes it before handing it on. Owners can share their old website as a reference.

Key decisions

01

WhatsApp onboarding with no sign-up

The owner messages a number, answers a few questions about their business, and gets a designed prototype back in the chat. Their phone number creates the account automatically, and they're asked for an email later. The first prototype comes out of the 15 free tokens.

Why: We considered a sign-up step before the first build and dropped it. Every extra step added friction between the owner and seeing their site appear, and that first prototype is what wins them over. Collecting details after the first build kept time to value as short as possible.

02

Questions about the business only

On the web, the owner types what they want to build. The agent asks specific follow-up questions about the business, then asks for the logo and the feel they want. The build starts once it has enough to work with.

Why: The owner knows their business, and we know design. The questions cover what only the owner can tell us, which keeps cognitive load low, and the agents handle every design decision.

03

A studio where the site builds in front of the owner

The chat sits on the left, and the site builds live in the centre and stays clickable throughout. An active agent panel shows which agent is working (research, art direction, content, SEO or build), and a version history below the preview lists every earlier version of the site.

Why: Watching the site take shape builds trust. Owners see a team of agents working for them, and that's a feeling we designed for on purpose.

04

A chat that only claims what's true

  • The chat confirms a change only after it shows up on the page. For photos, it checks that the image is actually on the site.
  • The progress bar follows real build events and stops short of 100% until the site is ready.
  • If a step runs longer than usual, the chat says so.
  • If a build fails, a clear error state appears and the owner's work is saved.
  • After each build, the chat offers more than one next step as options.

Why: The first version confirmed changes as soon as the agent made the request, before checking the page. That's what told the bakery owner her photos were up three times. Owners who can't judge the site themselves stopped trusting the chat after one false "done".

05

Building in the owner's own language

The chat works in Indian languages, so owners can describe their business and ask for changes in the language they run it in. The agents understand the request and build from it, and the owner never has to switch to English.

Why: Many of these owners don't work in English, and in the alpha test the agent misread requests written in their own language. An English-only builder would have shut out the owners Produsa was built for.

06

Tokens as the one unit of cost

Building, editing and hosting are all paid in tokens. The first site is free to build and publish with 15 tokens. After that, a build costs 10 tokens, and keeping a site live costs 10 tokens a month. The monthly plans are bundles of tokens with extras such as a custom domain. The owner sees their balance in the chat as they work, so they know what an action costs before they take it.

Why: Owners edit in bursts, not on a schedule, and a fixed monthly plan charged them the same whether they edited or not. With tokens, they pay for building, editing and hosting as they use them.

Results

  • 500+ builds in the first two months after the August 2026 launch
  • 290 websites shipped and live
  • Owners have started paying to keep their sites hosted
  • Many owners build their websites in their own language

Produsa has reached business owners in tier 2 cities who don't speak English, people that AI tools and website builders rarely reach. They describe their business in their own language, and they come away with a live website.

My role

I set the product direction and designed the UX and UI, the studio layout and the whole conversational experience. Our developer led development, and the two of us designers worked alongside him on the build. I built and debugged the agent orchestration, including the self-correcting and standards loops.

Reflection

  • Write the feedback rules before the first build. The "done" problem only showed up in usability testing. Next time, I'd decide what the chat is allowed to claim, and how it verifies each claim, before any agent runs.
  • A proven process made the agents easier to design. Every step in the pipeline already existed in agency work with real clients, so each agent had a clear job and a clear standard to check against.
